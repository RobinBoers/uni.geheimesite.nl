1. List the three paths in which the authors say neuroscience can help education.

- Neuroscience indirectly improves education by improving psychology theories.

- Neuroscience directly improves education, by biological (metabolic) factors that influence learning.

- Neuroscience indirectly improves education by providing self-knowledge of the brain, supporting meta-cognitive strategies.

2. Identify three or more pitfalls of “educational neuroscience” mentioned.

- Can only be relabeling terms from cognitive psychology with neuroanatomical names.
- Can be poorly communicates, thereby spawning myths.
- Can be used as after-the-fact justification for existing educational techniques (aka "window-dressing").
- Can be reductionist: explain everything at the lowest possible level, which is less applicable/relevant to educational practise.

3. What are two concrete, relevant insights for teachers/educators from educational neuroscience, according to the authors?

- The way we learn and forget differs per knowledge type (due to evolutionary biology), which is useful for educators to know, so they can adjust their teaching strategies accordingly.

- Learning is influenced by biological factords such as sleep, nutrition etc. which influence how the brain works. Understanding these properties means educators can adjust their teaching accordingly.

- Sensorimotoric, emotional and social processing have priority over cognitive processing. Educators need to ensure those to enhance/not impede learning.

4. For three empirical studies that the authors describe topic, method, results, and rate (0 = no relevance, 5 = high relevance) how relevant you
think the findings are for educational practice.

Rodriguez Buritica et al.

Topic: academic self-concept (with a behavioural and neuroscientific perspective)
Method: self-report and MCRI scans
Results: behavioural findings are interesting and useful, but cannot be linked to neuroscientific findings
Rating: 1, it might be useful to know how self-concept influences learning, but I feel like this was already known.

Menabò et al.

Topic: children's experience of bullying
Method: verbal self-report + eye-tracking
Results: self-report from children indicates they pay attention to bully and victim, but eye-tracking shows they are also aware of bystanders, and more specifically, defenders.
Rating: 4, these findings can be used in designing interventions/programs to deal with bullying.

Janssen & van Atteveldt

Topic: fixed/growth mindset & meta-cognition
Method: randomized trial with control group (and the neuroscientific method is using real-time 'experiential' neurofeedback)
Results: more students reported a growth mindset, and their scores dropped less than in the control group.
Rating: 4, the topic of the study is very relevant, because mindset is very important (self-efficacy etc.), but the results are a little disappointing (the intervention does not prevent burn-out or help low SES-students)
