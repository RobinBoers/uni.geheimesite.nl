## Community member choices

I chose the elderly resident and the investor/developer. I selected these because they represent two 'sides' of the story: the builders and the users of the city. And also because having two different ages, and two different requirements (comfort vs cost) seemed interesting. I chose a female American voice for the investor and a female Britisch voice for the elderly resident. The communication style of the investor was more formal and corporate; I feel like they more carefully selected words. The communication style of the elderly resident was by comparison way more direct and to the point. Another difference was that the investor actually tried to give input and search for ideas / solutions, while the elderly resident was mainly asking me for explanations.

For the investor, I tried to use their interest as a 'lever', if I could reframe their concern to actually _benefit_ their incentive/interest, I could convince them. The use of language was far more transactional. We talked about data, evidence, pilots, timelines, branding. It was more logic-based and less emotional.

For the elderly person, on the other hand, I tried to address concerns by proposing a concrete solution: here's a specific app, here's how to get to your daughter, here's how public transport can provide an alternative to car-centric society. I also tried to reassure her that cars weren't going a away _entirely_, and I tried to create an image of what a carless society might look like, to convince her the change was necessary and for the better. The conversation was based more on emotion in this regard, not on logic or data.

In both cases, I did not negotiate or compromise. Instead, I tried to reframe their concerns to address them, and then asked them for further input. This changes them from an opponent to a stakeholder. In many cases, opposition isn't necessarily about the plan itself, but more so not being seen in it. By asking for input, people feel included, and willing to look for a solution together.

It was easier to communicate with the developer, I think. This was mostly because they were more willing to respond and keep the conversation flowing. However, that conversation had a structural problem: the AI broke character almost immediately and became a co-designer rather than a genuine opponent. In a way, this is what I had intended, but it happened to quickly, I think. I got almost no time to actually argue with the AI. Therefore, I did not really need to _defend_ our plan as much. The elderly resident was harder to convince. This was mostly because that conversation could not be won easily with better arguments, but needed me to reassure her on an emotional level.

The elderly resident also raised an issue which we had not thought about much: what to do about accessibility? That is a real concern for any city, and especially relevant when designing a sustainable city. I think this is a value that we overlooked: inclusivity, allowing everyone to participate.

The "safe, low stakes" environment did not necessarily improve my language use. However, I think this is because I consider myself a competent speaker, and have confidence in my language profiency; I am not afraid to use or experiment with the English language in regular conversations. However, I do think this can be helpful for novice speakers.

An affordance that the AI has, which is not possible in a regular conversation, is the live transcription. This allows you to read back what you and the AI have said previously, which can be very helpful in formulating a response. It also helped me a lot, because I often struggle to hear what people are saying in noisy environments. The AI solves this with live subtitles.

I did not notice any particilar cultural biases in the way the AI talked. What I did find annoying is that whenever you paused for a little too long because you were trying to formulate a sentence, the AI would start replying immediately. This led to me and the AI to talk through eachother on multiple occasions. At some point I think I screamed 'SHUT UP' because it would keep talking at the same time as me.
