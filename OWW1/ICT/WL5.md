learning by creating multimedia

there is research on learning-by-teaching and peer-tutoring
(but nothing on making multimedia, which is not face-to-face)

examples of making:
- making a video
- writing a blog
- making a test question
- making a slow-mation (or stop-motion)

why does it promote learning?

- retrieval practise
(preparation -> making from memory)
- cognitive explanation
(generative processes create germane load; elaboration/monitoring)
(+ exposes knowledge gaps -- more metacognitive tho)
- social cognitive explanation
generative processes yay
but social presence as mediating/in-between factor

social presence = degree to which people are aware of audience
(from online learning, where it is related to increased participation)
(because if it feels alone, you might do less bc less motivation)

OR: via psysiological arousal (forms of stress)
there is a link between arousal and performance
and there is a link between arousal and determinants for learning (memory etc.)

(there is a threshold where it is no longer positive; then overwhelming)

SO:

- creating multimedia triggers retrieval practise. by retrieving information from ltm
  and processing it, we learn more. it gets more 'solidified' in ltm.

- creating multimedia makes use of generative processes. these kinds of cognitive processes
  facilitate learning by elaborating/monitoring own performance. and can also expose
  knowledge gaps.

- social presence has two influences:
    1. it increases motivation and participation, especially in online settings
    2. is creates psysiological arousal, which is a bodily state of 'activation'
    (aka ustress?) that is positively linked with a) performance and b) learning determinators(?)

does the audience matter?

conditions:
- restudy
- fictious audience (low social presence)
- real audience (high social presence)

real audience impaired learning compared to other conditions
^arousal through the roof -> stress consumes working mem resources

same goes for larger audience. (no effect on learning outcomes, but
the perceived larger audience does take more effort which does not pay of)

in line with social psychology:
if you know something well, having an audience improves performance, if you do
not know something well, having an audience impairs performance.

vlogging works better about conceptual knowledge (how does something work? relations?)
not so much for procedural knowledge (do this in these steps)

takeaways:
- study, vlog, restudy
- spoken > written
- explain from memory
- for conceptual knowledge, not procedural
- possibibly supplemented by learning-by-drawing
- avoid too high social pressure/presence
  (bc too stressful)

the effect is not merely a retrieval-practise, also a little
bit of generative processes and social presence.
