behaviourism

learning is behavioural change, shaped by stimuli and responses
blank slate, no genetics
mind is a black box, we cannot look into it (so who cares)
rewards and punishment to enforce behaviour

behaviourism outdated? (blank slate, black box etc)
but still everywhere: gamification (kahoot leaderboard, achievements, badges), drill-and-practice, feedback

cognitivism:
mental proceses, rather than observable behaviour

cognitive schemas, and integrating new information in those schemas

like a computer, input, processing, output. (just IO devices haha)

information processing model

input -> sensory mem (unattended lost) --(attention)-> short-term mem (unreheased lost) --(encoding and retrieval)-> long term mem

measure via physiology, eye tracking, EEG (breinscan) etc.

constructivism:
contextualized, more meaningful in realistic contexts
active process, learner *does* something
social activity, together with others
emphasis on motivation

authority more shared between students and teachers, so teacher more like support/guide/mentor/facilitator

examples: problem-based learning, inquiry-based learning, challenge-based learning

critique from cognitivists: approach does not work, because too little guidance
(^ richard clark, from the media thing)

examples in TEL: computer-supported collaborative learning (aka google docs)
experience "real-world" settings, but in controlled/safe environment (aka vr/ar)

lta: aligning principles with learning theories and tools
examples: behaviourism - feedback, cognitivism - prior knowledge actv, constructivism - collab

much overlap between cognivism, constructivism

also other learning theories, but less relevant
